# Reject b9380440: ASCII regression and helper placement sensitivity

The first complete quiet-host comparison rejects candidate
[`b9380440`](https://github.com/xipkit/vibescript/commit/b9380440ff7dba4d0c1d2721c24110405a84214a)
against baseline
[`7ab5ea12`](https://github.com/xipkit/vibescript/commit/7ab5ea12a4b87a438789a2a347b4a0d399a05f4d).
SIMD lone escapes improve, but SIMD ASCII lines regress about 5%, and portable
lone escapes retain a material helper-placement effect. The portable helper
also gains vector-mask reloads in its new span probe. This candidate must not
advance to performance acceptance.

## Complete physical-host experiment

Dumont ran this comparison on physical OTIS: Apple M4, macOS 27.0.1,
Rust 1.98.1 / LLVM 22.1.8, unchanged release profile. Each of four
SIMD/portable × base/head builds has two independent 32-placement sweeps:
move only `write_string` (`string`), then only `Output::put_run` (`run`),
in four-byte steps through 128 bytes. Every other original symbol stays fixed
within a sweep. Pairs match actual function addresses modulo 128. The
manifests preserve both writer and helper addresses; the fixed address of the
other function can differ between base and head. These are independent axes,
not a crossed experiment covering arbitrary joint layouts.

All 256 executables match full output and accounting for the three unlimited
and three metered fixtures. Three differential retired-instruction samples
per case and placement use 3,000/9,000 calls. All eight timing sweeps complete:
seeds 187 and 188 for each mode/axis, one full warmup cycle, 15 measured rounds,
6,000 calls per payload, randomized base/head/control order. Every group
includes a repeated baseline binary as a noise control. Across the experiment
there are 4,096 accepted groups (3,840 measured), zero rejected groups, and
peak accepted one-minute load 2.532, below the predeclared cutoff of 6.

The fixtures are 59 ordinary ASCII bytes followed by LF, repeated 69 times;
`é界🙂` repeated 456 times; and `ab` followed by LF, repeated 1,366 times.
Each string is the payload of `JSON.stringify({id: 7, payload: ...})`.
A separate native sample of the exact SIMD head confirms the lone-escape case
executes the expanded helper. Sampling finished before timing started.

## Assembly observations and the next intervention

The rejected source probes the next three bytes at every escape-loop entry to
find a nearby escape. ASCII lines take that extra path once per line without
using the batch; their SIMD instruction median increases about 2.9% and their
timing median about 5%. Reusing the already-computed short ASCII span length
is the next intervention. That is a source/assembly hypothesis to test, not
proof that these branches explain the complete timing difference.

The portable helper frame grows from 0xd0 to 0x150 bytes. Its new ordinary-span
probe uses the shared prefix classifier and reloads vector constants at
`0x1001bc0b0` (`ldp q3, q1, [sp,#0x40]`), `0x1001bc0f0`
(`[sp,#0x30]`) and `0x1001bc104` (`[sp,#0x20]`), with a possible
`run_prefix` call later in that path. The tight escape and byte/vector-copy
loop bodies have no stack reloads; that does not remove the repeated reloads
on visits to the span probe. The next helper uses a bounded scalar copy/probe
for short complete spans, leaving longer spans to the outer scanner.

Both modes' `run_prefix` and `unicode_blocks` retain only two stack references
each, at entry/exit. The portable outer writer grows from 0x160 to 0x170 bytes,
with a new x28 save at `[sp,#0x50]` and reloads on several paths. SIMD writer
frames remain 0x120. A complete path-by-path outer-writer spill audit is still
open: frame size or stack-reference counts alone do not establish hot-path
cost. The revised candidate needs a fresh audit of writer, helper, prefix,
and Unicode loops in both modes.

The independent sweeps do not isolate a hardware predictor mechanism, and
moving the work into a helper has not removed sensitivity. Further crossed
sampling of this rejected candidate is on hold. A new candidate must pass the
ASCII/Unicode guards and both full placement axes; a material helper effect
requires a predeclared crossed control before a broader layout claim.
